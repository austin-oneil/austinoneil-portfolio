## What does this cover?

My title at Prospecta Marketing is Senior SEO Specialist and Account Executive.
That is the real title and I am not going to dress it up. It also undersells the
job by a wide margin.

In practice I am the main technical point of contact for 30-plus accounts at a
time inside a book of more than 100, and the work is front-end development, CMS
architecture, custom plugin development, internal tooling and serverless
automation. It is a small team with almost no formal onboarding, so the answer
to "who handles this" has usually been whoever picks it up.

This page is an anonymized composite. Healthcare clients are not named here and
will not be.

## Recovering a launch that had already gone wrong

A large multi-location dental group came to us out of a site refresh that had
gone badly. I owned the CMS architecture and a full site redesign, and I worked
directly with their in-house engineering team rather than around them.

That last part is the whole thing. An agency arriving after a failed launch has
an easy story available: the last work was bad, hand it over to us. It is also
the story that guarantees friction with the people who will maintain the site
afterward and who know things about it that were never written down.

So I treated their developer as a colleague. We got on Zoom, we got in a room,
and we worked the problem together. Both the client and their own developer
credited the work directly, and I would rather have that than the win.

The conversion-focused location page layouts I built for them were reusable by
design, and the company ended up adopting them across other accounts.

## What did the numbers do?

Every figure below is from tracked visibility reporting, with the exact window
it happened in. No open-ended "since I took over" framing, because that hides
when things actually moved.

- A dental client from **Jun 2025 to Apr 2026**: search visibility from 29.9% to
  71.3%, the fastest sustained growth in the book.
- A practice at real risk of closing, **Feb 2024 to May 2024**: 5.4% to 28.3% in
  three months. That growth bought the owner enough time to sell the business
  with a patient base still attached, which mattered more than the number did.
- A client on organic SEO only, no paid, **Sep 2025 onward**: 74.6% to 90.1%
  over ten months. They paused the engagement because the patient influx got
  ahead of what the practice could take. That is the strangest compliment I have
  received at this job.
- Across the book: clients up 40% or more inside the first one to two months of
  dedicated work, landing pages moving from position 30-plus into the top ten
  inside a month, and 100% on quarterly review counts while carrying 30-plus
  accounts.

I also built the agency's answer engine optimization strategy from nothing:
entity-based optimization, knowledge-graph and brand-entity work, and
structured data aimed at AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity rather than at
ten blue links. Early results are good. It is too new for me to hand you a
number I would defend.

## Decisions

**Build the location pages once, properly.** Multi-location businesses are the
same page repeated with different content, which means the work is either done
once well or many times inconsistently. Designing for reuse is why the layout
traveled past the client I built it for.

**Say the title accurately and let the bullets carry the scope.** I could
describe myself as a developer at an agency and nobody would check. The title on
record says SEO Specialist, so that is what I put on things, and then I show
what the work actually was. A title that does not survive a reference check is
not worth the upgrade.

## Becoming the security lead

We had a hack that exposed credentials.

I ended up as the team's subject matter expert on it, which is a polite way of
saying I was the person who did not put it down. I built the remediation
strategy and executed it, and every client account was secured within 24 hours
with the exploited weak points closed.

**Why 24 hours and not a phased rollout.** With credentials exposed across a
shared book of accounts, the exposure window is the entire risk. A staged plan
leaves a known-compromised credential live on some accounts while you fix
others, and an attacker holding a list does not work through it in the order you
scheduled. Doing all of it inside a day cost a great deal more coordination and
removed the window completely.

That week is also the clearest example of the thing I keep claiming about
myself: the hard part was not the technical remediation. It was calling a lot of
people, telling them something bad had happened, and keeping them calm enough to
act on it quickly.
