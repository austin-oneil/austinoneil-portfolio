## What problem does this solve?

An organization tied to Colorado Avalanche alumni and coaches runs two related
things: Lacroix Hockey, a nonprofit youth hockey org, and Drill House Sports
Center, the affiliated training center.

I came in on the Drill House side after a prior developer left the work
incomplete, which meant the first job was not technical. The relationship needed
repairing before anything I built would be trusted.

They were also paying MindBody more than $600 a month for booking and payments on
a business whose booking needs were narrower than what that platform is built for.

## What constraints did it have to work inside?

Two properties with genuinely different needs. Lacroix Hockey is a nonprofit that
needed to be maintainable by people who are not developers. Drill House needed
custom booking behaviour that off-the-shelf platforms charge a lot to approximate.

Whatever replaced MindBody had to keep full functionality. A cheaper tool that
loses a feature the business depends on is not a saving, it is a problem deferred
to whoever handles the first booking it cannot take.

## What did I build?

Lacroix Hockey on Wix and Drill House Sports Center on WordPress, and custom
JavaScript booking and payment tools for Drill House replacing MindBody.

The booking tools cover custom flows for summer camps with waivers, extended
custom form fields, and both down-payment and monthly payment options.

## Decisions

**Move the nonprofit to a lower-cost platform rather than build them more.** The
recommendation that made the client money was not a build. Lacroix Hockey did not
need a custom site, it needed one that its own people could update without calling
me, so I guided them onto a platform that fit. Recommending less work than they
expected to buy is part of why the relationship recovered.

**Replace MindBody rather than negotiate it down.** The cost was not the whole
problem. Camp registration with waivers, extended fields, and split payment terms
were things the platform made awkward and expensive at the same time. Building
those flows directly meant the booking experience matched how the business
actually sells, and the cost fell out of that rather than being the goal.

**Two platforms on purpose, not by accident.** Running the nonprofit on Wix and
the training center on WordPress looks inconsistent from the outside. It is the
right split: one needs to be editable by volunteers, the other needs custom
booking logic that WordPress will host without fighting.

## Outcome

Monthly software cost went from more than $600 to $25, a 95 percent reduction,
with full functionality retained.
