## What problem does this solve?

A sports media company needed a front end built from scratch for two properties.
DNVR was the established one. PHNX was the first build outside the native market,
in Phoenix, carrying a large blog and ecommerce memberships.

I designed and built the full WordPress front end for both. The infrastructure
was owned by a separate AWS developer, and I coordinated with them across EC2,
Elastic Beanstalk, RDS, S3 and Route 53.

## What actually happened

48 hours before launch, the site was disconnected from AWS. The causes were
mixed-content issues and an incomplete setup. The original developer who owned
that infrastructure went dark.

I was the front-end developer. I was not the person who built the AWS side and I
was not the person on call for it, and that stopped being relevant the moment
nobody else was going to do it.

## What did I do?

I worked directly with the CEO to diagnose the failure, studied AWS under time
pressure to understand a stack I had coordinated with but had not built, and got
an emergency replacement developer brought in and oriented on the system in time
to launch.

It launched with hours to spare.

The important nuance, because it is the part that gets flattened in a retelling:
I was not the emergency hire. I was the person who found and oriented them.

## Decisions

**Diagnose before recruiting.** Bringing in a replacement developer with 48 hours
on the clock only works if they can start on the actual problem rather than
spending the first day discovering the system. Learning enough AWS to describe
what was broken and why was what made the handoff take hours instead of days.

**Escalate to the CEO directly.** With the infrastructure owner unreachable and a
fixed launch date, the decision that mattered was whether to move the date or
replace the developer, and that was not mine to make. Going straight to the person
who could make it is what left enough runway to act on the answer.

## What happened after

The relationship continued past the launch. I improved AWS security that had been
left weak, fixed front-end bugs across both properties, and worked on UX.
